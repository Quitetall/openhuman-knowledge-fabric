# Incident Report: Unattended Bin — Custody Breach

**Classification:** Restricted — Owners Only
**Incident date:** July 22, 2026
**Report prepared by:** Toby Flint, HR & Compliance
**CAPA owner:** Toby Flint

## Summary

On July 22, 2026, a locked collection console from a Scheduled Route
customer was left unattended in an unsecured area outside a Munder Diffin
plant loading dock for approximately 3 hours and 40 minutes before being
discovered and moved to secure holding. No evidence of tampering was
found, but the incident is classified as a chain-of-custody breach
because the console was outside a controlled area for longer than our
procedure allows.

The affected customer was notified per our incident notification
practice. The customer's name is withheld from this report per our
policy of not naming affected customers outside the minimum-necessary
notification and remediation records; that reference is retained
separately by Toby Flint's office.

## Root Cause

The immediate cause was a staffing interruption: the driver assigned to
move the console into secure holding was reassigned to an unrelated
mechanical issue before completing the unload sequence, and no backup
process caught the gap until the scheduled evening dock walk-through.

Contributing factors identified:

1. **No hard stop in the unload sequence.** The receiving process relies
   on one driver completing both drop-off and move-to-holding, with no
   independent check if that sequence is interrupted.
2. **Yard staging area is not badge-controlled.** The temporary staging
   spot outside the dock door has camera coverage but not access
   control, inconsistent with the intent of the Chain-of-Custody
   Procedure even though it is not, strictly, a public area.
3. **Dock walk-through frequency.** The 3-hour-40-minute gap before
   discovery exceeded our 1-hour walk-through target because
   walk-throughs had been informally reduced to twice per shift.

## Impact Assessment

- No evidence of unauthorized access, tampering, or seal breach was
  found in camera review.
- The seal was confirmed intact at the time of discovery and again at
  destruction.
- This is nonetheless recorded as a custody breach because the
  procedure's continuous-custody requirement was not met, independent of
  whether harm occurred.

## Customer Notification

The affected customer was notified within 48 hours of discovery, informed
of the delay, the confirmed seal integrity, and the completed
destruction, consistent with our notification commitment for any custody
exception. No customer materials were compromised.
