# Chain-of-Custody Procedure

**Owner:** Dwight Blunt, Operations Manager (compliance oversight: Toby Flint)
**Applies to:** All personnel who collect, transport, store, or destroy
customer materials
**Standard referenced:** NAID AAA–style chain-of-custody

This procedure defines the minimum custody controls required for every
job, regardless of service tier. It is the internal companion to the
Shredding Operations SOP and takes precedence where the two conflict on
custody-specific steps.

## 1. Custody Log Requirements

A custody log must be created for every job at the moment material is
collected, whether on paper (approved carbonless triplicate form) or in
the records platform's mobile app. Each entry must capture:

- Job number and customer account number
- Container ID(s) collected
- Date, time, and location of pickup
- Approximate weight or unit count
- Driver name and badge number
- Site contact name or signature, when available
- Seal number, if the container is sealed for off-site transport
- Time and method of destruction
- Name of the person confirming destruction

Custody logs are never left unattended with the materials they describe.
A completed log is scanned into the records platform within 24 hours of
job completion and retained for a **minimum of three years**.

## 2. Seals

- Any container leaving a customer site without on-site destruction must
  be closed with a numbered, tamper-evident seal before departure.
- The seal number is recorded on the custody log at the point of sealing.
- Seals are inspected and their numbers verified against the log at the
  plant receiving dock before the container is opened.
- A broken, missing, or mismatched seal is an automatic exception (see
  Shredding Operations SOP §7) and must be reported to Dwight Blunt and
  Toby Flint the same business day.

## 3. Two-Person Rule for Hard Drives and Media

Because electronic media often carries a small number of highly sensitive
records per item, hard drive and media handling requires two-person
verification at each of the following points:

1. **Collection:** Two authorized personnel (driver + site contact, or
   driver + a second crew member) jointly count and log each drive/media
   item by serial number or asset tag where available.
2. **Transfer to plant:** A second employee independently verifies the
   item count against the custody log before the load is accepted at the
   plant.
3. **Destruction:** Two employees are present at the point of physical
   destruction (shredding or crushing); one operates the equipment and
   the second confirms and initials the destruction log.

A single-person exception (e.g., staffing shortage) must be pre-approved
by Dwight Blunt and noted on the custody log with the reason.

## 4. Storage Prior to Destruction

- Materials awaiting destruction (same-day plant queue) are stored in a
  locked, access-controlled area of the plant, entry logged via badge
  swipe.
- No customer material may remain in an unsecured area, including a
  parked but unattended truck, at any time.
- Same-day destruction is the target for all plant-bound loads; any load
  held overnight must be logged with the reason and location.

## 5. Retention of Logs

- Physical custody logs are scanned and the originals filed by month at
  the plant office; digital entries are the system of record once scanned.
- Retention period: **3 years minimum** from the date of destruction,
  consistent with our published Terms of Service and NAID AAA–style
  standard.
- Logs are available to the relevant customer upon written request for
  audit purposes, redacted of any other customer's information.

## 6. Audits

Toby Flint's office performs a quarterly sample audit of custody logs
against destruction certificates issued in the same period, and reports
findings to Jim Miller and Hank Shredder.
